### 🧭 Project Background

The **Chuanchang River Gallery Bridge**, located in the Yannan High-Tech Zone of Yancheng, Jiangsu Province, is a hybrid project that combines a bridge and architectural complex spanning the Chuanchang River.  
In addition to serving as a transportation link across the river, the bridge integrates commercial and public functions on the deck, forming a multifunctional urban node that brings together **transportation, commercial facilities, and urban landscape**.

The bridge-top building has a total height of approximately **15.4 m**, consists of **3 floors**, and provides a total commercial floor area of around **6,000 m²**.  
The structure adopts a **steel structural system**, while the façade system is composed of **full glass curtain walls, aluminum panel curtain walls, and metal roofing panels**.  
The design goal was to satisfy both traffic and building load requirements while creating a landmark public space with strong urban identity.

- **Location**: Yannan High-Tech Zone, Yancheng, Jiangsu Province, China
- **Function**: Bridge + Commercial Complex
- **Structural System**: Steel structure
- **Curtain Wall Systems**: Full glass curtain wall, aluminum panel curtain wall, metal roofing system
- **Building Scale**: 3 floors, approx. 6,000 m²

### 🧩 BIM Modeling Workflow

#### 1. **Defining Scope and Modeling Objectives**

- Primary modeling scope: commercial building curtain walls (full glass, aluminum panels, metal roofing), purlin steel structures, and glass railings
- Secondary modeling scope: architectural structure and bridge body (beams, columns, walls, slabs)
- Clash detection focus: conflicts between curtain walls and structural beams/columns
- Fabrication requirements: dimensions, quantities, and positioning of steel purlins for construction takeoff

### 🔮 Summary & Reflection

- **Key Challenges**:  
  The most challenging aspect of this project was the design of the fish-tail-shaped architectural portion. It required a careful balance between visual expression and structural performance. In addition, the overall free-form geometry of the building made it critical to use parametric methods to assist construction while maintaining high precision and controlling construction costs.

- **Modeling Strategy**:  
  Non-repetitive and design-driven components were modeled manually based on CAD drawings to ensure efficiency. Repetitive and construction-assist components (main purlins, purlin extensions, aluminum alloy pipes, and fish-tail pipe structures) were modeled parametrically using **Rhino + Grasshopper** to achieve both high efficiency and high accuracy for fabrication support.

- **Construction Coordination**:  
  During construction, the BIM model was used for quantity takeoff and clash detection, successfully identifying interferences between steel structural joints and curtain wall systems, significantly improving on-site construction efficiency.

- **Lessons Learned**:  
  This project further deepened my understanding of the BIM engineer's workflow and highlighted key considerations before modeling begins. Through hands-on practice, I strengthened my proficiency in Grasshopper-based parametric design. Overall, the project enhanced my capabilities in modeling complex architectural forms and coordinating design with construction, reinforcing the concept of integrated design and fabrication.
