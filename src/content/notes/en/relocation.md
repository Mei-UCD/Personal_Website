It has already been more than a month since I finally settled down after the move. This time, because of a job change, I had to make a long-distance move. And honestly, it was exhausting..

About a month before the move, I started thinking about how to move everything as cheaply and efficiently as possible. Since I didn't have any large appliances or a bed to deal with, my original plan was to pack everything I owned and send it through **Yamato Transport** (the famous "Black Cat" delivery service).

There is, however, a size limit for this service: the total of the three dimensions (length + width + height) of each package cannot exceed a certain limit. Each package was quite cheap, and after a rough calculation, I estimated that I could probably ship all my belongings for a little over ¥20,000, except for my bicycle.

I looked into shipping the bicycle separately, only to find that it was surprisingly expensive—more expensive than the bicycle itself. Talk about overkill 💦. At that point, I figured that I might as well pay a little more and hire a moving company instead. At least I wouldn't have to disassemble and pack everything myself.

## Getting Quotes from Moving Companies

So, I made appointments with two of the major moving companies in Japan, **0123** and **Sakai**, to get estimates.

The main difference was that Sakai came to my place for an in-person estimate, while 0123 provided an online estimate. After experiencing both, I honestly didn't find much difference between the two. The service was pretty much the same: checking my belongings, calculating the moving distance, confirming the moving-out and moving-in dates, and then giving me a price.

Of course, once they gave me the initial quote, the most important part was to **negotiate the price** 😅.

Moving companies in Japan tend to quote surprisingly high prices, so this was when I had to put my negotiation skills to the test.

**Sakai** initially quoted me more than ¥120,000. I was like: excuse me?? 🤨

I had already done my homework on various websites and on Xiaohongshu, and from what I had seen, I thought I could probably get it down to around ¥50,000. I expected that even if the initial quote was high, maybe it would eventually come down to ¥70,000–80,000 at most. So getting a quote over ¥120,000 was quite a shock.

Anyway, once I heard the price, it was time to start acting. I put on my best "this is way too expensive" face, showed them examples of quotes other people had received for similar amounts of stuff and similar moving distances, and asked if they could bring it down to around ¥50,000.

Maybe because it was my first time moving in Japan and I didn't have much experience negotiating, we went back and forth for ten or twenty minutes, and I eventually managed to get it down to just over ¥70,000.

Then came the **0123** online estimate. After checking everything, they initially quoted me around ¥90,000. And once again, the performance began. I was already exhausted 😰.

The person on the other end started doing their own little performance too, frantically scribbling away on paper on the other side of the screen, before saying they could bring the price down to around ¥70,000.

I gave an awkward smile, and then came the critical question: **"What's your budget?"**

Looking back, I really hate myself for what came out of my mouth.

I said **around ¥60,000**..

Honestly, I probably could have gotten it for ¥50,000 if I had just kept my mouth shut.

Anyway, in the end, this company was a little over ¥10,000 cheaper than the other one.

## The Final Choice

As a poor person wandering around trying to make a living, naturally I chose the slightly cheaper option: **0123**.

Online reviews seemed to favor **Sakai** a little more, and before getting the quotes, I was actually leaning toward them. But since I couldn't negotiate the price down enough, I decided to let it go.

In the end, **0123** was actually pretty decent.

Although there was a little incident on moving-out day.

They had already moved everything out of the apartment, but I completely forgot about the bicycle that was still sitting in the bicycle parking area. For some reason, instead of checking the original quote and using it as a checklist, they simply asked me whether everything had been moved out.

And just like that, the bicycle was forgotten 🙃.

I hurriedly called them, and by that point, the truck had already left for more than an hour. I thought I might have to pay extra for them to come back and pick it up.

Fortunately, they didn't charge me anything extra.

That said, I still think this was partly a service issue on their side. Not using the original quote as a checklist seems rather unscientific to me. This was definitely not my fault.

## Moving In

Moving in was actually much smoother than moving out.

But the biggest problem was that Japanese apartments are just **so small**.

The moment everything was brought inside, the room became incredibly crowded. And I still had to buy a bed, some larger pieces of furniture, and appliances, which only made things worse..

There were boxes and stuff everywhere, and I literally had to look for gaps just to find somewhere to put my feet.

Cleaning and organizing everything was a nightmare. I had to be extremely careful not to bump into the floor or walls, otherwise I'd probably end up paying for the damage.

It was at this moment that I truly understood the importance of having a place you can call your own😫.

## A Little Advice

**When negotiating, don't tell them a higher budget than you actually have. If you already have a target price in mind, just say that. Otherwise, you won't have much room left to negotiate.**

I still regret this so much whenever I think about it.

Next time, I'll definitely start with:

> "もう少し安くなりませんか？"

Accompanied by an awkward but polite smile 😇
